Why are there a sun and a moon? A Korean brother and sister know

"The Sun and the Moon" is one of the best-known folktales in Korea. For hundreds of years, it has answered a big question: where did the sun and the moon come from? In Korean, it is often called Haenim Dalnim, a polite way to say "Sun and Moon."

In this story, Mother works at a party. In old Korean villages, neighbors helped each other with big feasts, like a wedding or a birthday. Rice cakes, tteok, were the special party food. Women often carried heavy loads on their heads, in a wide wooden bowl set on a little straw ring.

Every village had a well, and often a big old tree stood beside it. People came there to get water and to talk.

Like the old tale, this book says that the tiger ate the mother, but it does not show it. In the old tale, the tiger dies. Here, he falls into a sorghum field, susu in Korean, and is hurt. People say that is why sorghum stalks have red spots. The children are saved by being brave and clever, and by helping each other.

Look for the sun and the moon in Korean art, too. In old palaces, a painting of the sun, the moon, and five mountain peaks stood behind the king's throne. You can see it on Korea's 10,000-won bill.

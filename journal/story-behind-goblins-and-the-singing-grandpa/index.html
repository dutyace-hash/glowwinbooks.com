Are Korean goblins scary? Meet the dokkaebi

"The Goblins and the Singing Grandpa" is a funny Korean folktale. In Korean, it is called Hokburi Yeonggam, "the old man with a lump." Korean children love the moment when the goblins bang their clubs and shout, "Gold, come out! Ttukttak!"

Dokkaebi are Korean goblins. In old stories, they are not evil spirits. They are noisy, playful, and a little silly. They love singing, dancing, wrestling, and jokes, and they are easy to trick. Some stories say dokkaebi love to eat buckwheat jelly called memilmuk. People said that on dark nights you could see dokkaebi fire, little blue lights floating in the hills. In the oldest Korean stories and pictures, dokkaebi usually have no horns at all. They look like big, funny, hairy people, so that is how we drew them.

In old Korean stories, a quick, clever answer can fool a dokkaebi, just as the kind grandpa did. But people who are greedy and copy the trick usually end up in trouble.

Today, dokkaebi still show up in Korean picture books, cartoons, and TV shows. When Koreans make something very quickly, they sometimes say "ttukttak!" just like the goblins.

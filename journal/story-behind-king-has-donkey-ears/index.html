Can a bamboo forest keep a secret?

"The King Has Donkey Ears" is one of the oldest stories in Korea. It was written down more than 700 years ago in Samguk Yusa, a famous book of old Korean stories. In that book, the king is King Gyeongmun of Silla, an ancient Korean kingdom, who lived more than 1,100 years ago. The secret-keeper is the man who made the king's bokdu, a tall black hat worn at the royal court.

In the old story, the hat-maker kept the secret until he was very old. In this book, he cannot wait that long, and nobody is hurt or punished. The old story also says the king had the whispering bamboo cut down and planted cornel trees instead. But when the wind blew, the trees still whispered about his long ears! This book keeps that part. Then the king feels sorry, and he plants bamboo again.

If this story sounds familiar, you may know the Greek myth of King Midas, who also had donkey ears. People all over the world have told stories about a secret that is too big to keep.

When you read aloud, let your child be the wind. Each time the bamboo goes "Whoosh! Swish, swish, swish...", whisper the next line together: "The king has donkey ears!"

Daenamu is the Korean word for bamboo. Today in Korea, a "bamboo forest" is a playful name for a place where people can share a secret without telling who they are.

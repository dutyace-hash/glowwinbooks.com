Korean Folktales for Little Readers · Book 5

Heungbu and Nolbu

A Korean Folktale · Retold by Bright Lee

One kind brother, one greedy brother, and a little swallow who knows the difference.

Paperback on Amazon

Big brother Nolbu has a house full of rice, but he never shares. "Mine, mine, all mine!" Little brother Heungbu has a tiny straw hut, three hungry children, and a kind heart. When a baby swallow falls and hurts its leg, Heungbu's family wraps it, feeds it, and keeps it warm. The next spring, the swallow comes back with a tiny seed.

A huge round gourd grows on the roof. Back and forth, back and forth, saw, saw, saw! What is inside? And when greedy Nolbu wants a lucky swallow of his own, what will he find in his gourds?

A funny, warm story about sharing, kindness, and saying sorry, with a join-in sawing chant and bold, playful pictures in the style of minhwa, Korean folk painting.

A note for parents: In the old tale, Nolbu hurts a swallow on purpose. In this book, nobody hurts the swallow. Nolbu only wraps a big, silly bandage on a leg that was never hurt. When Nolbu's wife swings her rice paddle, only grains of rice fly, and Heungbu happily eats them. Nobody is hit, and the brothers end the story eating together at one table.

Korean Folktales for Little Readers · Book 8

Kongjwi and Patjwi

A Korean Folktale · Retold by Bright Lee

One broken hoe, four helpers, and a lost flower shoe that changes everything.

Paperback on Amazon

Kongjwi works from morning to night, while her stepsister Patjwi eats rice cakes and naps. When there is a big feast, Stepmother smiles. "You may come too, after you fill the water jar, take the husks off the rice, and weave this cloth." But the jar has a hole, the rice is a mountain, and the loom is so slow. Who will help Kongjwi?

A black cow, a toad, a flock of sparrows, and a fairy from the sky! Each one says "I will help you!" because Kongjwi was kind to them first. And at the feast, a magistrate holds up one little flower shoe. "I must find the girl who lost it."

A warm, funny story about kindness, hard work, trying again, and saying sorry, with a join-in "Who will help Kongjwi?" refrain and bold, playful pictures in the style of minhwa, Korean folk painting.

A note for parents: The long old version of this tale has sad and scary parts after the lost shoe. This book stops at the happy part. Kongjwi's mother is gone, and the book says so gently in one line. Nobody is hurt or punished. Patjwi pushes and squeezes but the shoe will not fit, then she comes to say sorry with a plate of rice cakes, and Kongjwi says, "Let's share!" In the old story Kongjwi marries the magistrate. Here she is a young girl, so he gives her a prize for being kind and hardworking.
